Pausa is a good app in a crowded niche that nobody has won yet

The core idea works and is backed by research. But in the last 18 months about 20 small iPhone apps launched that also pause shopping or takeaway apps, and none of them has real traction. Pausa wins this on story, name and UK focus, not on more features.

How good is the app?
7/10

The best-designed app in its niche, with a strong core loop and an honest privacy stance. It asks a new user to learn too much in week one.

How marketable is it?
6/10 → 8/10

Real UK pain and clear seasonal moments. Held back by a name that is already taken and a pitch spread across a dozen features.

The problem is real
9

The average UK adult makes about 42 impulse buys a year, around £944 (Vanquis, 2025). 39% say they impulse-buy when they're up late, tired and browsing.

The core loop works
8

A pause with an easy way out changes behaviour. In the one sec field study (PNAS, 2023), people dismissed 36% of app opens after the pause and tried to open those apps 37% less over six weeks.

Different from rivals
6

A real lock, plus "Park it till morning", plus a timed pass, plus an honest check-in is a rare mix. But Ordless, PauseBuy and Friction each cover half of it, and the Screen Time API makes copying easy.

Calm and simple
5

15 screens and about 12 ideas before the paywall. Four reward systems run side by side: XP, 18 rank tiers, 9 stickers and the calm-days card.

Privacy and trust
9

No account, no bank link, everything on the phone, no made-up savings figures. Most rivals show "money saved" numbers they can't check.

Look and feel
8

The tear-off ticket, receipt paywall and mascot stickers are distinctive. Most rivals look like generic budgeting apps.

Staying power
6

The most common complaint about breathing pauses is that people start tapping straight through them after about two weeks. The waiting room and treat nights help, but the pause itself never changes.

Price
7

£29.88 a year is mid-market: above one sec and CartPause ($19.99), level with Friction ($39.99), far below Opal ($99.99).

Name and findability
3

"Pausa: Screen Time & Focus" is already on the App Store, next to Pausi, Pauza, paus, PauseBuy, CartPause and Piggy Pause. A search for "pause" will not find you.

What this tells us

  • The money is in blockers with a sharp identity. BibleScroll, Brainrot and SocialLite run on the same Apple tools as dozens of plain blockers. They earn because each speaks to one person with one story.
  • Nobody owns spending yet. The 20 or so spending apps are side projects with a handful of ratings. That is the opening, and it is also a warning: the idea alone hasn't pulled people in.
  • Almost every rival is American. They name DoorDash, Walmart and Target. None is built around Deliveroo, Just Eat, Vinted, ASOS, Klarna and payday in pounds.
  • A hard paywall is the right call. Apps with one turn about 10.7% of trials into paying customers by day 35, against 2.1% for free-first apps, and keep yearly subscribers about as well (RevenueCat, 2026).

Positioning

Sell one moment: the 11 PM basket that waits till morning

Right now the pitch is "Shop on purpose" plus a dozen features. The most distinctive and most filmable thing Pausa does is keep the app shut tonight and ask again at 8 AM.

Suggested one-liner

Your 11 PM basket can wait till morning.

The late-night pause for UK shopping and takeaway apps. No bank, no account, no made-up savings.

Who
UK, 18 to 34, phone-first, ordering Deliveroo or Just Eat and browsing SHEIN, Temu, ASOS or Vinted late at night. Second group: people doing a no-buy or low-buy month.

Design changes

Eight changes, in the order to make them

Each "before" is a real screenshot of v18. Each "after" is a sketch of the idea, not a final design. These are recommendations only; nothing in the main build has been changed.

DO FIRSTMedium effort · biggest effect on sign-ups

1. Tell one story in setup, and unlock the rest during week one

Setup shows 15 screens and about 12 ideas before the paywall. Cut it to 8 screens with one idea: the pause, and Park it till morning. Rules, challenges, stickers, risky hours and the voice note then arrive one at a time on Today over the first week, each as a small "New" card.

  • Keep: hold to pause, welcome, the 11 PM scroll, Screen Time, Apple's picker, try the pause, one tour slide, paywall.
  • Move to week one: the other 5 tour slides, pain points and Set your rules.

This answers your "calm and simple" rule and the retention problem at once. New things appear right when the novelty of the pause starts to fade, around week two.

v18 setup, screen by screen

Two more tour slides (Shop with a plan, Earn stickers) are not shown and also move to week one.

DO FIRSTSmall effort · biggest effect on trust and reviews

2. Promise a reminder before the free week ends

Add a three-step timeline to the receipt paywall: today, a reminder on day 5, the plan starting on day 7. The reminder is on by default and is a simple notification from the phone.

Surprise charges at the end of a trial are the single biggest complaint in this category, about a quarter of negative reviews, and Opal takes most of the heat. Your audience is money-anxious, so this matters more for them than for anyone.

DO FIRSTSmall to medium effort · keeps people past week two

3. Keep the pause fresh with one true line

While the circle breathes, show one line that changes each time. Every line is something Pausa can check, so it stays honest:

  • "You left Uber Eats 4 of the last 5 times."
  • "2 things are parked till 08:00."
  • "Treat night is Friday, 2 days away."
  • Your own voice note from calm you.

The main reason people drop one sec is that the same breathing screen becomes invisible after about two weeks. A personal line is something you read, so the pause keeps working.

NEXTMedium effort

4. Give Today one job: "Am I protected, and what's waiting?"

Today currently holds the calm-days card, a three-row plan, a pass link and the test button. Make it a single status, a morning card for parked wants, and one button. The plan rows move to Settings under Your rules.

You set the rule "glanceable status on Today, controls in Settings". People open this app when they feel tempted or when the 8 AM reminder lands. Both moments need one answer, not a list.

NEXTSmall effort

5. Give XP when the plan is kept, not when shopping starts

Tapping "I need it now" shows a "+5 XP" chip on the pass screen, so the app pays you for choosing to shop. Drop that and put the reward on the check-in instead: +10 for any honest answer, +15 with checkout proof, as now.

This follows your rule that rewards must be earned. It also stops the odd message "well done for shopping" at the exact moment someone is trying not to.

NEXTSmall effort · copy only

6. A shield that knows it's late

On the shield, Apple only lets Pausa set the colours, icon, a title, a subtitle and two button labels. Use them for facts about tonight instead of a fixed line. After 22:00: "It's 23:10. Uber Eats will still be there at 8." During the day, the current goal line stays.

In the Whistl survey, 55% of under-24s blamed late-night browsing for their impulse buys. A message that names the hour and the app feels personal and fits inside Apple's limits.

LATERSmall effort · UK edge

7. Make setup feel built for the UK

Add one short screen before Apple's app picker that names UK apps and suggests shielding pay-later apps too. In the bank-limit guide, name UK banks with real spending controls: Monzo and Starling offer spending blocks, Barclays lets you switch off some spending, and Revolut has card limits.

  • "Tick Shopping & Food. Also worth shielding: Klarna, Clearpay and PayPal, under Finance."
  • Be clear that shielding the Klarna app doesn't stop Klarna at checkout inside a shop app.

Pay later became FCA-regulated on 15 July 2026, so it's in the news. Rivals are built for DoorDash and Walmart. This is cheap, and it's the difference people notice in the first minute.

LATERMedium effort · new screen

8. A Sunday recap with counts, never pounds

Once a week, one calm card: pauses, how many you left, parked and let go, passes kept to plan. It can be shared as an image. Fold the rank and sticker progress in here instead of showing four reward systems on Me.

It gives people a reason to open Pausa in a week with no temptation, which is when they forget it exists and cancel. MeanIt uses the same idea. Keeping it to counts respects your no-money-saved rule.

Risks

What could still go wrong, and what to do about it

High

The name

"Pausa: Screen Time & Focus" is live on the App Store. Pick a new name before spending anything on marketing, and check trademark and App Store search first.

Medium

App Review guideline 4.3

Onus is Android-only, so the "same developer, same app" rule is a smaller risk on the App Store. The bigger one is Apple calling blockers a saturated category. Lead the listing and screenshots with shopping and takeaways, choose Finance or Lifestyle, and explain the waiting room, pass and check-in in the reviewer note. This is based on developer forum reports; Apple's decisions vary.

Medium

Shields that fail silently

About 16% of complaints about blockers are about Screen Time permission resetting after an iOS update, so the app quietly stops working. Keep the "Shields on" pill honest, and warn loudly on Today if the permission is lost.

Medium

Easy to copy

Anyone can build a shopping blocker in a few weeks, and 20 people already have. The lasting edge is a UK brand, the look, and a steady stream of late-night content, not a feature list.

Watch

Renewals

Typical apps keep about 27% of yearly subscribers after a year, and health and habit apps have the weakest first renewal. The Sunday recap and week-one unlocks are there to lift that.

Watch

Price

£29.88 a year is fair against rivals. Test £24.99 a year in the UK only if trial-to-paid comes in well under 10%.
