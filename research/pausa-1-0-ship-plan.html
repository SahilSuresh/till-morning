Must add

Not in the build yet, and needed on launch day.

1. Ask for notifications in setup

The 08:00 "still want it?", the day-5 trial reminder, pass ending, the Sunday recap and the shield fallback all need notifications, and setup never asks. Add one screen after "try the pause": "We'll ask at 8 AM if you still want it."

Small

2. "Shields stopped working" warning

If Screen Time access is switched off or lost after an iOS update, show it on Today and send a notification. Silent failure is about 16% of complaints about apps like this.

SmallScreen Time

3. Leave cleanly

A "Turn Pausa off" row in Settings that removes every shield before someone deletes the app, plus a short help page. Opal's worst reviews are people still locked out after deleting it.

Small

4. App Store name and page

You're keeping Pausa as the brand, but Apple needs a listing name no other app uses, and "Pausa: Screen Time & Focus" exists. Something like "Pausa: Stop Impulse Shopping" works and carries search words. Plus icon, four screenshots, privacy policy and the "Data not collected" label.

MediumNot code

5. Apple's Family Controls approval

Request the distribution entitlement now, for the app and each extension that uses Screen Time (shield, shield buttons, activity monitor). Approval can take weeks and blocks launch.

Why smaller wins

Four reasons not to add more before launch

Features aren't what's missing in this market

About 20 rivals already have pauses, wishlists and savings trackers. None has traction. What decides it is the story, the name and getting in front of UK users.

Every extension is more Apple risk

Each extra extension needs its own Family Controls approval, and the Screen Time tools are known to be fragile. A small, reliable 1.0 beats a big buggy one.

Calm is the product

Your audience deletes apps that overwhelm them. With 1.0 they learn three things: the pause, the waiting room and the pass.

Updates are marketing

Shipping the held features one by one gives a "What's new" every few weeks and a fresh card in the app, right when the novelty of the pause fades.
